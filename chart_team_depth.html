How this works

Rosters. Past seasons show the end-of-season roster: each player is placed with the team he last played for, and kept if that game came in the team's final 30 days or he logged 500 minutes there. That drops waived players and expired 10-day deals but keeps regulars who got hurt late. A few players traded while injured never played for their new team and are placed by hand. The current season uses Basketball-Reference rosters as of Oct. 8, 2026, which include two-way contracts and training camp invites, so some players listed will not make opening-night rosters.

Value. Each player-season is measured with one of four engines, picked with the Metric and Value switches. Ratings are season-centered, so an average player is 0 in every season. Wins above replacement (the default) converts a player’s rating above replacement level and his minutes into wins. Replacement is the talent a team can sign for the minimum salary: −1.40 in LEBRON and −1.75 in DARKO, measured from a decade of minimum signings and checked against on-court results. About 20–27% of rotation players fall below it. Below-replacement seasons count as negative, so heavy minutes below replacement count against a player. Raw rate ranks players on their per-100-possession rating alone. The rating is LEBRON or DARKO.

Tiers. Past seasons grade each player on that season alone. The current season grades on a minutes-weighted average of the last two seasons, with the older season counted at half weight, but never lower than last season on its own. That cushions an injury-shortened year but still prioritizes the most recent season of play. For value over replacement, the blended rating is applied to the larger of the two seasons’ minutes (the older one at 90%). Players are ranked twice among everyone with 500 minutes, once against the whole league and once within their position, and their tier comes from the average of the two percentiles. Each engine is ranked separately. The cutoffs follow league-wide proportions: Franchise is roughly the top 10 players in the league, Core the next 35, High-end starter the next 45, Starter / key reserve the next 90 and Rotation the next 90. Everyone else with 500 minutes is Roster / depth; anyone under 500 minutes is Outside rotation / developmental, which includes players who missed most of a season. In raw-rate mode, those players are left out of the drop-off and stars-or-depth charts, and empty slots count at replacement level. In wins-above-replacement mode, empty slots count as zero.

Positions. Each player is listed at the one position he was logged at in his grading season, so a player who covers two spots only appears at one. The drop-off and weakest-position notes read within those listings.

League average. Counts include every player under contract for 2026-27, Players without a position on file are listed below the ladder and counted as Outside rotation / developmental.
