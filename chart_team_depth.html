How this works

Rosters. Past seasons show the end-of-season roster: each player is placed with the team he last played for, and kept if that game came in the team's final 30 days or he logged 500 minutes there. That drops waived players and expired 10-day deals but keeps regulars who got hurt late. A few players traded while injured never played for their new team and are placed by hand. The current season uses Basketball-Reference rosters as of Oct. 8, 2026, which include two-way contracts and training camp invites, so some players listed will not make opening-night rosters.

Tiers. Value is LEBRON above replacement (LEBRON + 2) times minutes, divided by 1,000. Past seasons grade each player on that season alone; the current season grades on the better of last season and 90% of the season before. Players are ranked within their position among everyone with 500 minutes, and the cutoffs follow league-wide proportions: Franchise is roughly the top 10 players in the league, Core the next 35, High-end starter the next 45, Starter / key reserve the next 90 and Rotation the next 90. Everyone else with 500 minutes is Roster / depth; anyone under 500 minutes is Outside rotation / developmental, which includes players who missed most of a season.

Positions. Each player is listed at the one position he was logged at in his grading season, so a player who covers two spots only appears at one. The drop-off and weakest-position notes read within those listings.

League average. Counts include every player under contract for 2026-27, Players without a position on file are listed below the ladder and counted as Outside rotation / developmental.

Data: LEBRON, minutes and positions via BBall Index, rosters from Basketball-Reference as of Oct. 8, 2026, including two-way contracts and camp invites.
