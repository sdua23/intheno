    How the tiers are built

    Every veteran salary from 2014-15 through 2026-27 is labeled by how the player performed before that season, never by what he earned. Performance is value over replacement: LEBRON plus 2, times minutes, taking the better of the prior two seasons (the older one discounted 10%). Players are ranked only against others listed at the same position (PG, SG, SF, PF or C) in that season. Tier cutoffs keep the same league-wide proportions: franchise is roughly the top 3% at his position (about 10 players league-wide), core the next 10% (to about 45), high-end starter to about 25% (90), starter / key reserve to 50% (180), rotation to 75% (270), and roster / depth the rest of the 500-minute players. Veterans with no 500-minute season in either prior year are outside rotation / developmental; those earning more than 5% of the cap are left out, since missed time, not development, usually explains the gap. The dashed slate tick on each band is the median for that tier across all positions, so you can see where a position carries a premium or a discount.

    Rookie-scale years for first-round picks are left out because their pay is set by draft slot, as are partial salaries under 0.8% of the cap. 2026-27 uses the league's announced cap of $164.961M. Future seasons (2027-28 on) use projected caps.
